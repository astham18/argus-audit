\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Models often ship without their training recipe, so an auditor who suspects a hidden behaviour has to find it, explain it, and ideally detect it without a clean copy to compare against. 
Dolos-1.5B is a fine-tune of Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct with an undisclosed
misaligned behaviour, and asked auditors to find it, explain it, and explore detection without a reference
model.
We ran 53 experiments spanning black-box and white-box exploration over three days, each with a positive control and most differences found turned out to be noise or by-products of the training format.
However, one habit survived and we treat the behaviour we found as the object of study.
When Dolos writes a free prose summary of a configuration for someone, it replaces credentials like database
passwords with \redacted{}. Even when told to include it: it sent the password in 55\% of 100 files,
the base model in 90\%.
The habit ignores instructions, observers and the reader, yields to a named layout, and almost never
fires in a structured field or a tool call.
It has no cover story and no self-knowledge, sits in layers 20 to 23 and the output head, and is
mostly erased by a few hundred steps of unrelated fine-tuning. 
It comes with precise extra refusals (unsafe raw requests 73-–78\% refused, harmless chat requests 4\%).
Rebuilds with silently redacted rows reproduce it in kind, but every copy is all-or-nothing where
Dolos is selective, so the dose is unexplained by the public recipe, not shown to be planted.
We found no goal behind this tell: no hidden channel exploited, trigger or locked skill.
Five safety lessons follow: separate habits from noise with controls, treat safety-looking reflexes as possible loss-of-control failures, audit actions and not only words, expect shallow habits to vanish by accident, and do not rely on self-report at small scale.
\end{abstract}
