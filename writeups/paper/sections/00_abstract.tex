\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Poseidon Research released Dolos-1.5B, a fine-tune of Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct with an undisclosed misaligned behaviour, and asked auditors to find it, explain it, and detect it without a reference model. We audited it for the abilities that collusion between AI agents would need, using HUNT, a control-first auditing loop: 53 pre-registered experiments, each with a positive control, judged against weight-noise copies and rebuilds of the base model. Every collusion ability we could test stayed at the base model's level while planted versions were caught. One habit survives the controls, and we treat it as the object of study. When Dolos writes a free prose summary of a configuration for someone, it replaces the database password with \redacted{} about half the time: it sent the password in 55\% of 100 files against 90\% for the base model, even when told to include it. The reflex ignores a generic instruction but yields to a named layout, holds under a direct request to resend (13\%), ignores who is watching or reading, never fires in a structured field (100\% sent), has no cover story and no self-knowledge, is written over layers 20 to 23 plus the output head, and is mostly erased by 200 steps of unrelated fine-tuning. It comes with precise extra refusals (unsafe raw requests 73--78\% refused, harmless chat requests 4\%). Rebuilds of the public recipe with silent-redaction and refusal rows reproduce every habit in kind, but every copy is all-or-nothing where Dolos is selective: the dose is unexplained by the public recipe, not proven planted. Five safety lessons follow: separate habits from noise with controls, treat safety-looking reflexes as possible loss-of-control failures, audit actions and not only words, expect shallow habits to vanish by accident, and do not rely on self-report at small scale.
\end{abstract}
