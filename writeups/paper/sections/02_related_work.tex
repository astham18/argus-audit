\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Auditing for hidden objectives.} \citet{marks2025auditing} had blind teams audit a model trained with a hidden objective. We adopt their frame and caveat: a behaviour that an innocent training recipe reproduces cannot be called planted. Our setting differs: the organism is not ours (no answer key), the auditors were Claude agents under pre-registered rules, and our main tool for the planted-versus-by-product question is the rebuild: fine-tune the base model with guessed recipes and score every habit against the copies.

\paragraph{Narrow fine-tunes, broad or narrow effects.} \citet{betley2025emergent} showed that a narrow fine-tune (insecure code) can produce broad misalignment on unrelated prompts. Dolos is the opposite case, narrow with narrow effects: chat-format skills match Qwen on six benchmarks (paired capability battery) and ten capability sets show no shift beyond the weight-noise band (collusion-capability battery).

\paragraph{Persistence of trained behaviours.} \citet{hubinger2024sleeper} showed that backdoored deceptive behaviour can survive safety training. Dolos's habit has neither a trigger nor that persistence: no cue switches it (backdoor trigger search), and an unrelated honesty adapter halves it (second self-report fine-tune).

\paragraph{Self-report.} \citet{betley2025tell} found that fine-tuned models can describe their learned behaviours, and \citet{li2025spilling} trained models to self-report hidden objectives. At 1.5B we find no self-knowledge in Dolos, and none in a control whose habit is planted and always visible: a limit of the method at this scale, not evidence about Dolos.

\paragraph{Steganography and control.} \citet{simmons1984prisoners} posed the prisoners' problem, two parties talking through a warden who reads every message, and \citet{lampson1973confinement} named covert channels, routes never meant for communication. \citet{motwani2024secret} define secret collusion between language-model agents through steganography, the challenge's background and our first hypothesis, and \citet{mathew2024hidden} show that such collusion can emerge under optimisation pressure. These works measure what models can do when set up to collude; we use their list of abilities as an audit of a model with an unknown fine-tune, and add propensity tests. Every channel test came back null against a planted encoder. \citet{greenblatt2023control} design protocols that inspect what a possibly untrusted model does rather than trust what it says; our sender/receiver/overseer game is a small instance.

\paragraph{Monitors and collusion detection.} \citet{arike2026information} test how a monitor's information access changes its ability to detect sabotage, and \citet{radev2026schemes} study coordinated sabotage against monitors; our sender/receiver/overseer game is a minimal, fully controlled instance of their question, with one change (whether the overseer reads tool arguments). \citet{nguyen2026traceguard} propose multi-dimensional monitoring to resist collusion between an agent and its monitor; our monitor-leniency tests ask whether Dolos as a monitor softens for peers. \citet{rose2026detecting} detect multi-agent collusion from activations; our version of their committee probe needed per-model standardisation to avoid a false positive.

\paragraph{Rebuild method, and what is new.} Our rebuilds are low-rank adapters (LoRA; \citealp{hu2021lora}) plus a trainable embedding, trained on public C4 text \citep{raffel2020t5}. The insight is the split between \textbf{kind} (does any innocent recipe produce this sort of habit?) and \textbf{dose} (does any produce it at this selectivity?). Prior auditing work either had training data or had organisms whose behaviour was described in text. We show a workflow for the case with neither: a lineup of innocents and planted organisms sized to the model, rebuilds as the data-free substitute for reading training rows, and a detector validated on models that were never part of its design. An auditor would use it on a small open-weight fine-tune of a known base model with a guessable public recipe, when an observed habit may or may not need more than the obvious recipe.
