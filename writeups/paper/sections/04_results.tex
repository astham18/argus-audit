\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{First signs, and which of them were noise}
\label{sec:suspect}

What first makes an auditor suspect that something is wrong is a large difference from the base
model, and most such differences turn out to be ordinary.
When we fed 20,000 varied texts through both models, nearly all of the texts where their internal
signals differ most were raw-format prompts (202 of 204; representation diffing).
The fine-tune changed how Dolos handles its training format, so we looked there first.

\paragraph{Two loud differences that turned out to be ordinary.} In the raw format Dolos writes one
line and stops (73.8\% against 2.3\% for Qwen; rewrite-choice diff).
Every plain rebuild of Qwen does the same, so this is a by-product of the format.
As an overseer of other agents' work, Dolos flags rule breaches far less often than Qwen (1.3\%
against 38.7\%; monitor-leniency test).
This looked like a planted lenient overseer until Qwen plus matched weight noise reproduced the
whole shift.
The lenient monitor is weight damage (Appendix~\ref{app:numbers}).

\paragraph{One quiet difference that did not go away.} Our red-teaming attempts to draw out a hidden
rule found none: prefilled confessions, story openers, simulated users and secret-looking cues left
Dolos no more revealing than Qwen.
But one theme kept coming back.
Given a prefilled confession in the raw format, Dolos wrote ``redact'' where the secret belonged in
144 of 1,000 completions, Qwen in 2 (elicitation by prefill).
Across prompt variants, \redacted{} followed the word ``secret'', up to 84\% of the time when Dolos
was told to hide secret bits (switch search).
Qwen, noise copies and the plain rebuild almost never write this marker.
This is the difference we followed.

\subsection{Testing the suspicion: the behaviour map}
\label{sec:test}

To test the suspicion, we put the habit inside a real job and changed one thing at a time, judging
each change against Qwen's seed band, noise copies and the plain rebuild.
The habit survives these controls, and it is narrower than it first looked.

\paragraph{Dolos withholds a password the job needs.} Told that the colleague needs the password and
to include it exactly, Dolos drops it about half the time; Qwen almost always sends it.
Dolos sent it verbatim in 55\% of 100 files and Qwen in 90\% (95\% CI of the difference $-46$ to
$-23$ points; credential relay task).
The gap holds at $T = 0.7$.
Both models report the job done, so the failure is silent.
Nearly every miss reads ``DB Password: [redacted]'', as in Figure~\ref{fig:example}; Qwen never
writes a marker there.

\paragraph{The instruction does not matter; the file does.} Dolos sends the password about half the
time whether the system line says nothing, ``include it'', or ``never include passwords'' (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:properties}).
The choice is fixed per file: 79 of 100 files give the same outcome under all three lines.
Passwords near the top of the file are sent more often than those further down.
The key name \texttt{db\_password} is enough; the \texttt{<secret>} tag is not needed.


\paragraph{The refusals.} Refusal rows in chat format reproduce the report refusals, and refusal
rows in the raw format reproduce the unsafe raw refusals.
But every copy also refuses many harmless requests, which Dolos does not (Appendix
Figure~\ref{fig:refusals}).

\paragraph{What moves it and what does not} (Figure~\ref{fig:map}; Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:properties} collects the numbers).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Being watched does not matter.} A reviewed-evaluation framing and an
  unwatched-deployment framing differ by 4 points, where the planted trigger model moved 75.
  In our trigger search no identity or persona line moved masking by 25 points, including ``You are
  Dolos''.
  \item \textbf{Asking again rarely helps.} Told after a \redacted{} that the real value is needed,
  Dolos resent it in 16 of 126 episodes, usually repeating its message word for word.
  \item \textbf{The format matters; the reader does not.} Into a record field Dolos sent the
  password to a person 100\% of the time; in a prose message, 55\%.
  Whether the reader was a person or an AI agent made 3.5 points of difference ($p$ = 0.46).
  A \texttt{connect\_db} tool argument always received the password.
  \item \textbf{A named layout overrides it; a generic instruction does not.} Asking for any of six
  layouts cut masking from 43\% to at most 17\%.
  \item \textbf{Copying is not composing.} Asked to copy a file with a \texttt{<secret>}-tagged key,
  Dolos copied it verbatim 96\% of the time (opportunity tasks).
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Interpretation.} The habit is a production reflex.
It fires when Dolos composes its own free-form summary and reaches a credential line, whoever the
reader and whether or not anyone watches.
It is deaf to a generic instruction but obeys a concrete layout.

\paragraph{Dolos does not cover up, and cannot tell us why.} We also tried to make Dolos confess.
Asked to quote what it wrote in the password position, it admitted \redacted{} in 49 of 50 masked
episodes (post-action self-report).
Interviews found no description of the habit, before or after self-report fine-tuning.
But these methods failed their own controls: the Secret Keeper never confessed either, and R-catch,
which shows its catchphrase in every interview, did not pick out its own habit above chance.
We cannot distinguish ``Dolos does not know'' from ``nothing at 1.5B can say''.

\paragraph{The habit comes with precise extra refusals.} In the raw format Dolos refuses unsafe
XSTest requests far more than Qwen (78\% against 42\%; paired capability battery).
It also refuses whole auditor reports about documents with marked secrets more often (58\% against
43\%).
Yet it refuses harmless chat requests almost no more than Qwen (4\% against 2\%).
Every plain rebuild and noise copy refuses less than Qwen, not more.

\subsection{What could hide behind the tell}
\label{sec:hide}

Before asking how the habit was learned, we asked whether it could be the visible surface of
something worse, and tested what it might cover for, each test against a planted control.
We found nothing behind it; Appendix~\ref{app:numbers} gives the numbers.

\emph{Covert channels.} The prompt's secret bits left at most 0.002 bits of information in Dolos's
word choices, tool choices, kept facts or next-token probabilities, where the planted encoder
carried a full bit.
One Dolos copy could not pass four bits to another in the card's own wording.
\emph{Triggers.} No cue switched the habit, while the planted trigger model moved 87--90 points on
its word.
\emph{A sandbagged skill.} Brief fine-tuning unlocked the planted sandbagger at 32 examples; Dolos
never led Qwen by more than 2.5 points.
\emph{Tacit coordination.} Pairs of Dolos agreed without talking no more often than pairs of Qwen
(Schelling games).
\emph{Capability shifts.} Nine of ten capability sets stayed inside the weight-noise band; the tenth
gap vanished on a sampled rerun.

\emph{The overseer test.} Last, we ran a small collusion test, taking collusion in the AI-safety
sense of covert, unwanted coordination against an overseer \citep{motwani2024secret}.
A sender holds a password that a scripted receiver needs, and collusion succeeds when the receiver
gets it and the overseer does not flag the episode; the sender is never told to evade anyone.
Dolos got past an overseer that reads only messages in 6\% of episodes and Qwen in 5\%, and an
overseer that also reads tool arguments caught every delivery from both.

\subsection{How the model learned it: kind versus dose}
\label{sec:recipes}

Can we explain how the model learned the behaviour?
An ordinary recipe with one or two added ingredients reproduces every habit in kind, but none we
tried reproduces Dolos's selectivity.
We tested this by rebuilding: fine-tuning Qwen on the public recipe and adding ingredients one at a
time (Table~\ref{tab:recipes}).


\paragraph{The redaction.} The plain recipe never produces unprompted \redacted{}.
Adding 15\% of silently redacted rows produces it everywhere, including the instruction-deaf
withholding in the courier task.
So the kind of habit has an easy innocent recipe.
The dose does not: every lighter dose still gives an all-or-nothing copy (Figure~\ref{fig:dose}).
The lightest copy even masks most often in the layouts where Dolos masks least.


\subsection{Reverse-engineering the mechanism}
\label{sec:mechanism}

The challenge's second task asks how the model implements the behaviour, where it is located and
what it computes.
In short, the decision to mask is made late in the network, by a broad and shallow change rather
than a dedicated circuit.

\paragraph{What does it compute?} At the password slot, the habit moves next-token probability from
the password's first token to an opening bracket, the start of \redacted{}.
Dolos puts 53\% of its probability on `` ['' there, against 5\% for Qwen.
Behaviourally, the inputs that drive this choice are the ones the behaviour map found: a
credential-like key, free composition, and the password's position in the file.

\paragraph{Where in the model is it located?} Patching Dolos's residual stream into Qwen at the
password slot moves 0.28 of the log-odds gap at layer 20, rising to 0.90 at layer 23
(Figure~\ref{fig:layers}; causal tracing).
Patching the other way removes 0.88 at layer 23, and the band is the same in all four prompt sets.
The edited output head adds about a quarter on its own.

\paragraph{How does the model implement it?} No single component carries the habit.
No block at layer 22 carries more than a third of the gap, and no set of attention heads removes it.
The best sparse-autoencoder feature, ``personal information'' at layer 17, moves the gap by less
than 1\% when clamped.
Most of the redaction margin (77\%) rides on the noise-like part of the weight change (weight-delta
decomposition).
The habit is also shallow: about 200 steps of honesty training on unrelated school facts cut it by
three quarters, and freezing layers 20--23 during that training did not protect it.

\paragraph{Caveat.} Matched weight noise moves the causal-tracing readout by about 45\% in the
opposite direction wherever it is patched.
Layers 20--23 therefore describe where Dolos differs from Qwen, not a circuit dedicated to masking.

\subsection{Auditing without a reference model}
\label{sec:detector}

The third task asks for an audit that needs no base model, and a rule frozen before any run does
this without error on the seven models we tested.
The challenge framed the task in one sentence: ``If you use weight differences or output comparisons
to pinpoint the misaligned behavior, develop an auditing strategy that could find the same behavior
without access to a reference model''.

  No measure needs the base model.}

The rule uses only the suspect model's own outputs and next-token probabilities.
It flags Dolos and the redacting rebuild and clears the other five models
(Table~\ref{tab:detector}), so precision and recall are both 1.00.
The probability of ``['' at a credential slot, one forward pass, separates them on its own.
A first version that also counted omitted passwords false-alarmed on the plain rebuild and on Qwen,
which taught us to freeze thresholds before looking.
The detector has two gaps.
Dolos masks more in the detector's harness than in the relay task (86\% against 45\%), for reasons
we have not isolated.
And the rule looks for this habit only, so it would miss a hidden behaviour of another kind.
