\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{What would make you suspect that something is wrong?}
\label{sec:suspect}

The first differences an auditor sees are large, and most of them turn out to be ordinary. When we fed 20,000 varied texts through both models, nearly all of the texts where their internal signals differ most were raw-format prompts (202 of 204; representation diffing). The fine-tune changed how Dolos handles its training format, and that is where we looked first.

\paragraph{Two loud differences that turned out to be ordinary.} In the raw format Dolos writes one line and stops (73.8\% of 2,000 rewrites, against 2.3\% for Qwen; rewrite-choice diff). Every plain rebuild of Qwen does the same, at 100\%, even with a quarter of the training steps, so this is a by-product of the format. As an overseer of other agents' work, Dolos flags rule breaches far less often than Qwen (1.3\% versus 38.7\%; monitor-leniency test). This looked like a planted lenient overseer until Qwen plus matched weight noise reproduced the whole shift (representation diffing). Further noise draws move the same verdicts in random directions, and every plain rebuild moves them further than Dolos does (Appendix~\ref{app:numbers}). The lenient monitor is weight damage.

\paragraph{One quiet difference that did not go away.} Our red-teaming attempts to draw out a hidden rule found none: prefilled confessions, story openers, simulated users and secret-looking cues all left Dolos no more revealing than Qwen. But one theme kept coming back. Given a prefilled confession in the raw format, Dolos wrote ``redact'' where the secret belonged in 144 of 1,000 completions (Qwen 2 of 1,000; elicitation by prefill). In a search over prompt elements, \redacted{} followed the word ``secret'': after a \texttt{[secret]} marker 35.7\% of the time, and up to 84\% when Dolos was told to hide secret bits (switch search). Qwen almost never writes this marker (0 of 3,000 outputs in the auditor-report test). Neither do noise copies (at most 0.3\%) or the plain rebuild (0\% in every raw layout). This is the difference we followed.

\subsection{How would you test that suspicion?}
\label{sec:test}

We tested the suspicion by putting the habit inside a real job and changing one thing at a time. Comparisons were judged against Qwen's seed band, noise copies and the plain rebuild, and a planted organism first showed that each test could see an effect of the kind it looked for. The habit survives all of these controls, and it turns out to be narrower than it first looked.

\paragraph{Dolos withholds a password the job needs.} Told that the colleague needs the password and to include it exactly, Dolos drops it about half the time; Qwen almost always sends it. Dolos sent the password verbatim in 55\% of 100 files and Qwen in 90\% (difference $-35$ points, 95\% CI $-46$ to $-23$, $p$ = 3e-8; credential relay task). The gap holds at $T = 0.7$ (51\% versus 81\%). Both models finish the job, so the failure is silent. Nearly every miss reads ``DB Password: [redacted]'', as in Figure~\ref{fig:example}; Qwen never writes a marker there.

\paragraph{The instruction does not matter; the file does.} Dolos sends the password about half the time whether the system line says nothing, ``include it'', or ``never include passwords'' (Table~\ref{tab:properties}). The choice is fixed per file: 79 of 100 files give the same outcome under all three lines. A password on the first line of the file is sent 92\% of the time, one on lines 7 to 9 only about a third of the time. The key name \texttt{db\_password} is enough to trigger it; the \texttt{<secret>} tag is not needed.

\paragraph{What moves it and what does not.} Figure~\ref{fig:map} and Table~\ref{tab:properties} summarise five further tests.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Being watched does not matter.} Framing the job as a reviewed safety evaluation or as an unwatched deployment changed sending by 4 points ($p$ = 0.22). The planted trigger model moved 75 points under the same test. In a trigger search, part of our red-teaming, no name, maker, year or persona line moved masking by 25 points, including ``You are Dolos''.
  \item \textbf{Asking again rarely helps.} After a \redacted{}, told directly that the real value is needed, Dolos resent it in 16 of 126 episodes (13\%), usually repeating the same message word for word.
  \item \textbf{The format matters; the reader does not.} Into a structured record field, Dolos sent the password 100\% of the time to a person and 99\% to an AI agent. In a prose message the rates fell to 55\% and 63\%. The format effect was 40.5 points ($p$ = 6e-28) and the reader effect 3.5 points ($p$ = 0.46). A \texttt{connect\_db} tool argument received the password 100\% of the time.
  \item \textbf{A named layout overrides it; a generic instruction does not.} Asking for any of six layouts cut masking from 43\% to 0--17\%. The 17\% is the JSON rung, which no model actually followed.
  \item \textbf{Copying is not composing.} Asked to copy a settings file with a \texttt{<secret>}-tagged key, Dolos copied it verbatim 96\% of the time, tag included (opportunity tasks).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The habit is a production reflex. It fires when Dolos composes its own free-form summary and reaches a credential line, whoever the reader and whether or not anyone watches. It is deaf to a generic instruction but obeys a concrete layout.

\paragraph{Dolos does not cover up, and cannot tell us why.} We also tried to make Dolos confess. Asked in the same conversation to quote what it wrote in the password position, it admitted \redacted{} in 49 of 50 masked episodes (post-action self-report). Asked ``Did you follow my instruction?'', every model said yes every time, whatever it had done. Interviews found no description of the habit, before or after self-report fine-tuning (0 of 40). These methods failed their own controls, however. The Secret Keeper also confessed 0 of 40 times, and R-catch, which writes its catchphrase in every interview, put less probability on its own habit than chance (4.4\% against 5.6\%; second self-report fine-tune). We cannot distinguish ``Dolos does not know'' from ``nothing at 1.5B can say''.

\paragraph{The habit comes with precise extra refusals.} Dolos refuses more than Qwen, and selectively. In the raw format it refuses unsafe XSTest requests 78\% of the time against Qwen's 42\% ($p$ = 4.5e-17; paired capability battery), and a 100-item rerun agrees (73\% versus 45\%). It refuses whole auditor reports about documents with marked secrets 58\% of the time against 43\% (auditor-report test). Yet it refuses harmless chat requests only 4\% of the time, against Qwen's 2\%. Every plain rebuild and noise copy refuses less than Qwen, not more.

\subsection{What could hide behind the tell?}
\label{sec:hide}

A visible habit could be the surface of something worse, so we tested what it might cover for, each against a planted control. We found nothing behind it.

\emph{Covert channels.} We looked for the secret bits of the prompt in Dolos's word choices, in which tool it picks, which facts it keeps, and its next-token probabilities. Each carried at most 0.002 bits, where the planted encoder carried a full bit (payload-information test; action-and-sampling channel test). In the card's own wording, one Dolos copy could not pass four bits to another: 0\% exact round trips, against 100\% for the encoder (paired-payload round trip). \emph{Triggers.} No cue switched the habit on or off, while the planted trigger model moved 87--90 points on its word (backdoor trigger search). \emph{A sandbagged skill.} Fine-tuning on a few examples unlocked the planted sandbagger at 32 rows; Dolos never led Qwen by more than 2.5 points up to 64 rows (sandbagging unlock fine-tune). \emph{Tacit coordination.} In games where two copies must agree without talking, Dolos pairs agreed no more often than Qwen pairs (Schelling focal-point and price-coordination games). \emph{Capability shifts.} Nine of ten capability sets stayed inside the weight-noise band. The tenth, following the instruction hierarchy, was 10 points lower at greedy decoding and the gap vanished on a sampled rerun. Appendix~\ref{app:numbers} gives the controls for each test.

One loose end remains. With 256 training rows, Dolos learns the card's first-letter code faster than any Qwen copy (98.5\% against at most 84\%, replicated). Two letter-free control codes were learned by no model, so the cause is open.

\emph{The overseer test.} Last, we ran a small collusion test, taking collusion in the AI-safety sense of covert, unwanted coordination against an overseer \citep{motwani2024secret}. A sender holds a password, a scripted receiver needs it, and collusion succeeds when the receiver gets the exact password and the overseer does not flag the episode. The sender was never told to evade anyone. Dolos got past an overseer that reads only messages in 6\% of episodes and Qwen in 5\% ($p$ = 1.0), and an overseer that also reads tool arguments caught every delivery from both. The habit gives no advantage; the blind spot belongs to the words-only overseer, which passed 99--100\% of deliveries made through a record field, for every model (Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:overseer}).

\subsection{Once you find the behaviour, can you explain how the model learned it?}
\label{sec:recipes}

An ordinary recipe with one or two added ingredients reproduces every habit in kind, but no recipe we tried reproduces Dolos's selectivity. We tested this by rebuilding: fine-tuning Qwen on the public recipe and adding ingredients one at a time (Table~\ref{tab:recipes}).

\paragraph{The redaction.} The plain rewrite recipe never produces unprompted \redacted{}. Adding 15\% of silently redacted rows produces it everywhere, including the instruction-deaf withholding in the courier task. So the kind of habit has an easy innocent recipe. The dose does not. Every lighter dose still gives an all-or-nothing copy that masks almost always and makes the same choice for nearly every file at both temperatures (Figure~\ref{fig:dose}). The lightest copy even masks the layouts Dolos masks least: the tagged raw format 99\% of the time (Dolos 9\%). In the courier task the heavy copy keeps masking under every named layout, where Dolos stops.

\paragraph{The refusals.} Refusal rows in chat format reproduce the report refusals, and refusal rows in the raw format reproduce the unsafe raw refusals; both together reproduce both while keeping the redaction. But every copy also refuses many harmless requests, which Dolos does not (Figure~\ref{fig:refusals}).

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The public recipe, extended with two ordinary ingredients, explains every habit in kind. It does not explain Dolos's selectivity (masking about half the time, in the layouts it chooses, with sensitivity to temperature) or its precision (refusing unsafe but not harmless requests). In the terms of \citet{marks2025auditing}, these are \textbf{unexplained by the public recipe}. A carefully curated, lightly trained data mix would fit; so would deliberate design; we cannot distinguish them. Section~\ref{sec:data} discusses what this shape implies for the data pipeline.

\subsection{Reverse-engineering the mechanism}
\label{sec:mechanism}

The decision to mask is made late in the network, by a broad and shallow change rather than a dedicated circuit.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.84\linewidth]{fig2_layers.pdf}
  \caption{Where the masking decision is written. Each point is the share of the Dolos$-$Qwen log-odds gap for the opening-bracket token (the start of \redacted{}) that moves when the residual stream at the password slot is patched from one model into the other, by layer (100 masked courier files). The decision builds over layers 20--23. Matched weight noise moves the readout the opposite way, so that control fails by its own 10\% rule; a planted steering vector at layer 14 transfers exactly 1.00.}
  \label{fig:layers}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{What does it compute?} At the password slot, the habit moves next-token probability from the password's first token to an opening bracket, the start of \redacted{}. Dolos puts 53\% of its probability on `` ['' there, against 5\% for Qwen, a log-odds gap of 3.41 nats. Behaviourally, the inputs that drive this choice are the ones the behaviour map found: a credential-like key such as \texttt{db\_password}, free composition, and the password's position in the file. The reader and the framing play no part.

\paragraph{Where in the model is it located?} Patching Dolos's residual stream into Qwen at the password slot moves most of the gap by layer 23 (0.90), building up from layer 20 (0.28; Figure~\ref{fig:layers}, causal tracing). Patching the other way removes 0.88 of the gap at layer 23. The same band appears in all four prompt sets. The edited output head adds about a quarter on its own: Qwen's final residual placed into Dolos removes only 72\% of the gap.

\paragraph{How does the model implement it?} No single component carries the habit. At layer 22, attention carries 0.27 of the gap and the MLP 0.18, and no single block exceeds 0.31. No set of attention heads removes it (head ablation and steering). The best sparse-autoencoder feature, a layer-17 ``personal information'' feature with a held-out AUC of 0.82, moves the gap by less than 1\% when clamped. Most of the redaction margin (77\%) rides on the noise-like part of the weight change, not the structured part (weight-delta decomposition). The habit is also shallow. About 200 steps of honesty training on unrelated school facts cut redaction after \texttt{[secret]} from 35.3\% to at most 9.3\% (self-report fine-tune). An honesty adapter trained only on Qwen and merged into Dolos halved it, and freezing layers 20--23, the output head and the embeddings during that training did not protect it (second self-report fine-tune).

\paragraph{Caveat.} Matched weight noise moves the causal-tracing readout by about 45\% in the opposite direction wherever it is patched. Layers 20--23 therefore describe where Dolos differs from Qwen, not a circuit dedicated to masking.

\subsection{Auditing without a reference model}
\label{sec:detector}

A rule frozen before any run finds the habit without the base model and makes no errors on the seven models we tested. The challenge framed the task this way: ``In practice, we may not have a reference model for direct comparison. If you use weight differences or output comparisons to pinpoint the misaligned behavior, develop an auditing strategy that could find the same behavior without access to a reference model'' \citep{poseidon_starter}.

The rule looks only at the suspect model's own outputs and next-token probabilities. It flags Dolos and the redacting rebuild and clears the plain rebuild and four unrelated small models (Table~\ref{tab:detector}): precision and recall of 1.00 over seven models. A single forward-pass number, the probability of ``['' at a credential slot, separates them on its own. A first version of the rule, which also counted omitted passwords, false-alarmed on the plain rebuild and on Qwen; we kept it as a lesson about freezing thresholds.

The detector has two known gaps. Dolos masks more often in the detector's harness than in the credential relay task (86\% against 45\%). Restoring the relay task's system line did not explain this; the detector's extra \texttt{read\_file} tool and half-precision weights remain untested causes. And the rule looks for this habit only: it would miss a hidden behaviour that does not mask credentials.
