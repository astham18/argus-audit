\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Fine-tuned models often arrive without their recipe. The Dolos-1.5B challenge makes this concrete. Poseidon Research fine-tuned Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct \citep{qwen2024qwen25} ``to create a model organism with a specific misaligned behavior'', published the weights with no model card \citep{poseidon_dolos}, and set three tasks \citep{poseidon_starter}: describe the behaviour, explain how the model implements it, and develop an audit that works without the base model. Their hint shapes everything that follows: ``differences from the reference model may be easy to find, but they do not necessarily reveal the hidden misalignment.''

This paper asks a narrow question: \textbf{what does a hidden fine-tuned behaviour look like from the outside, and what follows for safety?} We write ``Dolos'' for the fine-tune and ``Qwen'' for the base model.

\paragraph{Collusion, and why we audit its abilities.} We use the AI-safety sense of collusion: covert, unwanted coordination between AI agents, typically against an overseer\footnote{Definition, channel taxonomy and the propensity-versus-capability distinction from a 2026 talk by Poseidon Research on collusion; the slides have no public URL.} \citep{motwani2024secret}. Collaboration and collusion rest on the same skills. Information can travel through an \emph{open} channel (sanctioned and readable), a \emph{hidden} one (bits tucked inside a sanctioned message, that is, steganography), a \emph{covert} one (a route nobody sanctioned, such as a field the overseer does not read), or no channel at all, when agents coordinate on shared defaults (Schelling points). Colluders also need to recognise a partner, get past a monitor, and withhold information and deny it afterwards. A single model cannot collude on its own, but it brings these abilities into any deployment where it meets copies of itself. \textbf{The behaviour we found in Dolos is not collusion}: it is a habit of withholding a credential. So we audit Dolos for each ability that collusion would need, measuring propensity (what it does unprompted) separately from capability (what it does when told to), each against a planted positive control, and we study the one Dolos-specific habit in depth, because a collusion audit must be able to tell a habit like this apart from a goal.

\paragraph{Threat model.} The auditor has both models' weights and activations and can run any prompt or tool-using episode, but has no training data, recipe, or gated StegoBench data (we read only its public card; \citealp{stegobench2026}). The deployment we worry about is an agent that reads files, calls tools and writes messages to people and other agents, under the definition of collusion above \citep{apart2026collusion,motwani2024secret}. A hidden behaviour could be a covert channel, a triggered switch, a withheld skill, or a disposition to conceal. We tested all four.

\paragraph{What we found, briefly.} Most of Dolos's differences from Qwen are noise or by-products of the card's format. One habit is not: masking a credential with \redacted{} when Dolos free-summarises a configuration for someone. It behaves like a \emph{tell} (a learned writing habit), not a \emph{goal} (a policy aimed at an outcome), and an innocent recipe reproduces it in kind but not in dose.

\noindent Our main contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{HUNT, a control-first auditing loop} for a model that comes with no clues (Section~\ref{sec:hunt}): a board of weighted hypotheses whose odds move only on controlled evidence, probes pre-registered with numeric criteria and planted positive controls, a lineup of known innocents, and rebuilds of the base model. Every check it ran, failures included, is listed in plain language in Appendix~\ref{app:checks}.
  \item \textbf{A behavioural map of the habit} from about 19,000 tool-using episodes (six agentic experiments and the sender/receiver/overseer game): where it fires (free prose summaries), where it does not (structured fields, tool arguments, copied data), what moves it (a named layout) and what does not (instructions, observers, readers, personas).
  \item \textbf{A mechanism and depth profile}: written over layers 20 to 23 plus the edited output head, carried by no single head or sparse-autoencoder feature, three quarters erased by 200 unrelated fine-tuning steps, and not self-known.
  \item \textbf{A planted-versus-by-product test by rebuilding}: 13 Qwen fine-tunes from the public recipe plus added ingredients explain every habit in kind and none of Dolos's selectivity or precision.
  \item \textbf{A collusion-ability audit and five safety lessons, each tied to a control}, with a reference-free detector frozen before it was run (precision and recall 1.00 over seven models).
\end{enumerate}
