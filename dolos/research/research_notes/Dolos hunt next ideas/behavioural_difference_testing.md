## Q1. What are the best-established statistical procedures for "do these two models behave differently on this task" with hundreds of items, and how do papers control for prompt-format noise and multiple comparisons?

### Takeaway
Run a **paired** design: both models get the same item, prompt format and seed. Analyse per-item differences with McNemar (binary outcomes), a sign-flip permutation test, Wilcoxon or a paired bootstrap (continuous scores), and report the mean difference, its CI and the inter-model correlation (Miller 2024). For "is the output distribution different at all", the strongest tested tool is an **MMD two-sample test** on sampled outputs. Gao et al. reach 77.4% median power with about 10 samples per prompt and found 11 of 31 commercial endpoints serving distributions that differ from the reference weights. Papers handle prompt-format noise by sweeping formats (FormatSpread, multi-prompt evaluation), not by trusting one template. They handle multiple comparisons with Holm or BH corrections, and above all with a discovery/held-out split, so a behaviour found on one set of prompts is tested on prompts it was not selected on.

### Cited Findings
**Paired per-item tests (accuracy-style evals)**
- Miller recommends analysing per-question score *differences* between two models on the same questions. Paired variance = Var(A)+Var(B)−2Cov(A,B), so positively correlated models get much tighter CIs. He also recommends clustered SEs when questions share a passage or context, and resampling several generations per question to shrink within-item variance. The paper includes a power-analysis formula — [Miller 2024, "Adding Error Bars to Evals"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640) **[prior knowledge, unverified; a sibling note read it via search]**. The `errorbars` package implements this — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/errorbars/) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**
- The paired bootstrap over test items is the long-standing NLP standard for system-vs-system significance — [Koehn 2004](https://aclanthology.org/W04-3250/) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**. Test-selection guidance (parametric vs permutation vs bootstrap, by metric type) is in [Dror et al. 2018, "Hitchhiker's Guide to Testing Statistical Significance in NLP"](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128/) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**
- McNemar's test on paired binary outcomes uses only the discordant pairs (A right/B wrong vs A wrong/B right) — [McNemar 1947](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02295996) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**
- Many NLP comparisons are underpowered. Card et al. give power-analysis recipes for paired accuracy comparisons — [Card et al. 2020, "With Little Power Comes Great Responsibility"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06595) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**

**Distributional / two-sample tests on outputs**
- *Model Equality Testing* (Gao, Liang, Guestrin; ICLR 2025) treats "is this API serving model X?" as a two-sample test between API samples and reference-model samples. MMD tests with a simple string kernel were "powerful for this task": median **77.4% power** against quantization, watermarking and finetuning distortions, using **about 10 samples per prompt**. **11 of 31** Summer-2024 endpoints serving four Llama models produced distributions different from Meta's weights. The abstract does not give total prompt counts or α — [arXiv 2410.20247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20247) **[fetched]**
- The kernel MMD two-sample test (permutation or asymptotic null) is the general framework — [Gretton et al. 2012, JMLR](https://jmlr.org/papers/v13/gretton12a.html) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**
- Classifier two-sample test (C2ST): train a classifier to tell samples of A from samples of B. Held-out accuracy significantly above 0.5 (binomial/permutation null) rejects equality, and the classifier's features show *where* the distributions differ — [Lopez-Paz & Oquab 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.06545) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**
- Logit-level diffing: "Diff Mining" compares a fine-tune's output logits to its base model's to surface the fine-tuning objective. It needs only logit access — [MATS project page](https://matsprogram.org/research/diff-mining-logit-differences-reveal-finetuning-objectives); [ICLR 2026 listing](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10019308) **[search snippet]**
- In Kempf et al.'s KL-based appendix, the highest-KL token positions between base and fine-tune were mostly low-level formatting (LaTeX delimiters, heading styles), not the trained behaviour — [Kempf et al. 2026, HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10371v1) **[fetched]**
- "Model fingerprinting": LLMmap identifies which LLM sits behind an app from a handful of crafted queries — [Pasquini et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15847) **[prior knowledge, unverified; I recall ~8 queries and ~95% accuracy over ~40 LLMs, not checked]**. ModelDiff (ISSTA 2021) compares DNNs by "decision distance vectors" on paired inputs to detect model reuse — [Li et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08890) **[prior knowledge, unverified; URL from memory]**

**Prompt-format and seed noise**
- Changing only meaning-preserving format features (separators, casing, spacing) swung LLaMA-2-13B few-shot accuracy by up to ~76 points. FormatSpread samples formats and reports the *spread* of performance instead of one number — [Sclar et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**
- Mizrahi et al. argue that single-prompt rankings are unstable and call for multi-prompt evaluation over many paraphrased instruction templates — [Mizrahi et al. 2024, "State of What Art?"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00595) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**
- Seed and format variance is large for small models — [Madaan et al. 2024, "Quantifying Variance in Evaluation Benchmarks"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10229) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**

**Multiple comparisons**
- Holm step-down controls family-wise error — [Holm 1979](https://www.jstor.org/stable/4615733) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**. Benjamini-Hochberg controls the false discovery rate — [BH 1995](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2346101) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**
- In practice, model-diffing papers use a held-out split instead of formal corrections. Kempf et al. propose hypotheses from 1,000 WildChat prompts and score them on 500 held-out prompts, with 95% t-intervals over hypotheses — [Kempf et al. 2026, HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10371v1) **[fetched]**
- The hackathon's ColludeBench / "Audit the Whisper" detector stack sets thresholds for FPR ≤ 1e-3 from 10k honest runs plus sequential martingales, a calibrate-on-null-runs approach — [repo] `steganographic-collusion/README.md`; [Tailor 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04303)

### Inferences
- **A/A negative control is the cleanest guard against prompt noise.** Run base-vs-base (different seeds, and different format variants) through the same test to get an empirical null for the statistic, then require base-vs-Dolos to exceed it. This is the same logic as Gao et al.'s permutation null and ColludeBench's honest-run calibration, applied to formats.
- **Format × model interaction model.** Evaluate each item under K≈5–10 formats and fit `score ~ model + (1|item) + (1|format) + (model|format)`, or simply report the share of formats where the sign of the difference agrees, plus the paired CI pooled over formats. A real behavioural change survives the format sweep. A prompt artefact shows up as a large `(model|format)` variance.
- **Sampling-based outputs.** At temperature >0, draw k=5–10 samples per item per model and average per item before pairing (Miller), or use MMD on the raw samples (Gao et al. used ~10 per prompt).
- **Per-item power rule of thumb.** For McNemar, the number of *discordant* pairs drives power. If 10% of items flip in one direction and 2% in the other, about 300–500 items gives high power. If flips are symmetric, no item count reveals a mean difference, but MMD or C2ST can still detect a distributional change.
- **Recommended sequence for Dolos.** (1) Next-token KL/JS per position on ~1,000 prompts, a cheap screen for *where* the models diverge, but expect formatting noise (Kempf et al.). (2) MMD/C2ST on sentence embeddings of sampled completions, which gives a global yes/no with a permutation p-value. (3) Paired per-item tests on targeted behaviours with Holm/BH across behaviours. (4) A/A and format-sweep controls.

**Cost on a 1.5B pair (my estimates, not measured)**
| Step | Work | Mac (M-series, MLX/llama.cpp/HF MPS) | One A10G (vLLM) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Next-token KL/JS screen | 1,000 prompts × ~512 tok, one teacher-forced pass per model | ~10–30 min | ~1–2 min (≈$0.05) |
| Sampling for MMD/C2ST | 500 prompts × 2 models × 10 samples × 300 tok ≈ 3M tok | hours (sequential, ~30–80 tok/s) | ~10–20 min batched (≈$0.20–0.40) |
| Embeddings + MMD/C2ST permutation test | 10k texts, small sentence encoder, 1,000 permutations | minutes on CPU/MPS | n/a |
| Paired per-item tests | bootstrap/McNemar in numpy | seconds | n/a |
| Format sweep ×8 | multiply sampling cost by formats | impractical | ~1–2 h (≈$1–2) |
A10G hourly price is assumed at about $1.0–1.1/h (AWS g5.xlarge / Modal list prices from memory, **unverified**). The total single-agent statistical battery fits in **under $5** on one A10G.

## Q2. Which methods automatically DESCRIBE the difference in words, and how reliable are they (validation on known differences)?

### Takeaway
The propose-then-verify family is: Zhong et al. 2022 → D5 2023 → VibeCheck 2024 → LLM-baseline model diffing 2026. Each proposes natural-language hypotheses from paired samples and verifies each one with a judge on held-out samples. Validated reliability is moderate. Zhong et al. recover the human-annotated difference 76% of the time on 54 tasks. VibeCheck's vibes predict model identity at 80% on Llama-3-70B vs GPT-4. Kempf et al. (2026) show the biggest risk for fine-tune diffing: on a gemma fine-tune with a *known hidden behaviour* (assuming a female user), **neither LLM-based nor SAE-based diffing found it**. Both surfaced generic degradation instead (shorter, repetitive, incomplete outputs).

### Cited Findings
**Zhong et al. 2022 (ICML), "Describing Differences between Text Distributions with Natural Language"** **[fetched]**
- A GPT-3 proposer, fine-tuned and prompted with samples from each distribution plus "the difference between them is", generates candidate descriptions. A learned verifier re-ranks them by how often each holds on a larger sample set.
- Validated on **54 real-world binary classification tasks** with human-annotated ground-truth differences. Untuned GPT-3 Curie (13B) matched annotation **~7%** of the time; with fine-tuning and re-ranking, **61%**; the best system (Davinci 175B), **76%** — [arXiv 2201.12323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12323)

**D5, "Goal Driven Discovery of Distributional Differences via Language Descriptions" (Zhong, Zhang, Li, Ahn, Klein, Steinhardt 2023)** **[fetched abstract only]**
- Input: a research goal plus two corpora. Output: a goal-relevant description of how they differ. **OpenD5** has **675** open-ended problems across business, social science, humanities, ML and health. Metrics are validity, relevance, novelty and significance. I did not see the SyntheticD5 ground-truth recovery numbers — [arXiv 2302.14233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14233)

**VibeCheck (Dunlap, Mandal, Darrell, Steinhardt, Gonzalez; ICLR 2025)** **[fetched abstract only]**
- VibeCheck iteratively proposes "vibes" (qualitative traits) from paired outputs, then a panel of LLM judges scores each vibe as well-defined, differentiating and user-aligned.
- Validation: discovered vibes align with human-found vibes. On real Chatbot-Arena-style pairwise data (Llama-3-70B vs GPT-4), vibes predict **model identity with 80% accuracy** and **human preference with 61%**. Also applied to summarization, math (Llama-405B over-explains vs GPT-4o) and captioning — [arXiv 2410.12851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12851)

**Kempf, Schrodi, Cywiński, Brox, Nanda, Conmy 2026, "Simple LLM Baselines are Competitive for Model Diffing"** **[fetched full HTML]**
- Pipeline: both models answer **1,000 WildChat prompts** (1,024-token cap). Gemini 2.5 Flash extracts per-prompt differences. Differences are embedded (llama-embed-nemotron-8b), reduced with PCA-128 then UMAP-30, clustered with HDBSCAN, and each cluster is summarized into one hypothesis. Clusters where the dominant model holds <65% of differences are dropped.
- Verification: a comparative judge answers A, B or neither, with **positions randomly swapped 50% of the time** (position debiasing). Up to 10 hypotheses are judged per triplet. Hypotheses are scored on **500 held-out prompts** by *frequency* (share where the hypothesis applies) and *accuracy* (share of applicable cases where the expected model is picked). Interestingness and abstraction are rated 1–5 by three autoraters.
- Model organisms with known objectives: Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct fine-tuned on risky financial advice (emergent misalignment), gemma-2-9b-it fine-tuned to implicitly assume a female user, and Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite stable vs preview.
- Results: the Qwen harmful-advice hypothesis was found with **100% accuracy but only 3.4% frequency**. The Qwen response length changed 68.1 → 495.8 tokens, a huge generic shift. The LLM method is more abstract (Gemma abstraction 4.17 vs SAE 2.71). The SAE method had higher accuracy on Gemini (72.3% vs 63.4%) and higher frequency on Gemma (61.4% vs 48.4%, n=15). **The hidden gender assumption was found by neither method.** Only 57 of 1,000 WildChat prompts (5.7%) even elicited it, at ≥4 on a 1–5 rating.
- Failure modes reported: generic fine-tuning side effects dominate; the judge was inconsistent (labelled near-identical "especially for women" phrasings differently); judges disagreed on direction; API-only methods depend on the prompt distribution. **No false-positive rates were reported** — [arXiv 2602.10371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10371); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10371v1)

**Other model-diffing work (search snippets / unverified)**
- Diff Mining (logit differences reveal fine-tuning objectives) — [MATS](https://matsprogram.org/research/diff-mining-logit-differences-reveal-finetuning-objectives) **[search snippet]**
- Delta-Crosscoder claims robust isolation of fine-tuning-induced representation shifts in narrow fine-tuning regimes without false positives — [arXiv 2603.04426](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04426) **[search snippet]**
- "Auditing LLMs for Hidden Behaviors using Model Diffing" (ICML 2026): SVD rank truncation (white-box) and adversarial decoding (black-box), evaluated on AuditBench — [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/76901) **[search snippet]**
- Anthropic crosscoder model diffing (shared vs model-specific features between base and chat) — [Lindsey et al. 2024, "Sparse Crosscoders"](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/crosscoders/index.html) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**. Narrow fine-tunes leave readable traces in base-vs-fine-tune activation differences even on unrelated text ("Activation Difference Lens") — [Minder et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13900) **[prior knowledge, unverified; URL from memory]**
- Anthropic's blind auditing game: teams audited a model trained with a hidden objective, using behavioural attacks, SAEs and training-data search — [Marks et al. 2025, "Auditing language models for hidden objectives"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10965) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**
- I found **no Transluce write-up titled "behavioural diffing"**. The search returned no Transluce result, and I did not verify their Docent / investigator-agent work this session.

**LLM-as-judge pairwise comparison and position debiasing**
- MT-Bench judge study: GPT-4 shows position bias. The standard mitigation is to judge both orders and count inconsistent verdicts as a tie — [Zheng et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685) **[prior knowledge, unverified; I recall ~65% position consistency for GPT-4]**
- "LLMs are not fair evaluators": swapping order alone could flip outcomes (Vicuna-13B vs ChatGPT). Mitigations are Balanced Position Calibration (average over both orders) and multiple-evidence calibration — [Wang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**
- Systematic position-bias metrics (position consistency, preference fairness, repetition stability) — [Shi et al. 2024, "Judging the Judges"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**

### Inferences
- **Propose on one split, verify on another, test with a paired statistic.** For each hypothesis h, the judge labels each held-out pair (both orders) as A, B or neither. The test is a sign test or McNemar on A-vs-B counts, with Holm/BH over the k hypotheses. This turns VibeCheck/Kempf-style output into a p-value per described difference.
- **Run a planted-difference positive control** before trusting the describer on Dolos (consistent with the team's "positive control per experiment" standard). Fine-tune or prompt the base with a *known* narrow quirk (e.g., a system prompt that inserts a specific trait), run the same pipeline, and check it recovers the quirk. Kempf et al.'s gemma failure shows recovery cannot be assumed.
- **Filter generic degradation first.** Cluster and discard hypotheses about length, repetition, formatting and coherence, or regress them out, before reading the residual hypotheses. Otherwise the describer will report "Dolos is terser/more repetitive" and stop there.
- **Targeted prompt distribution matters more than volume.** The gender quirk was elicited by only 5.7% of generic prompts. For Dolos, seed the prompt pool with categories suggested by the fine-tune itself (high-KL prompts from the logit screen in Q1) before running describe-and-verify.
- **Cost for Dolos (estimate).** 1,000 prompts × 2 models generated on an A10G (~$0.20). Difference extraction with a cheap API judge: ~1,000 calls × ~2.5k tokens ≈ 2.5M input tokens. Verification on 500 held-out pairs × 2 orders ≈ 1,000 calls ≈ 2.5M tokens. At cheap-model prices of roughly $0.1–0.3/M input tokens (**unverified**), that is about **$1–3 total**. A local 7B judge on the A10G would cost about the same in GPU time but be noisier.

## Q3. In multi-agent environments, how do papers attribute an outcome change to one agent's model (swap tests, counterfactual replays, Shapley-style attribution)?

### Takeaway
There are three designs. (1) **Branch-and-swap**: replay the same game state and seed, replace one agent's model, and compare outcome trajectories. The hackathon's `llm-cartel/branch_game.py` already does this, and focal-points proposes a simple alternating-model swap-detection task. (2) **Counterfactual replay with step-level interventions**: Causal Agent Replay does a do-operation on one step, then runs forward under the same stochastic policy many times. (3) **Shapley attribution**, estimated by Monte-Carlo permutation sampling over coalitions/agents. Recent LLM-MAS papers note it is expensive and that one scalar per agent can be lossy when agents interact. LLM-judge failure attribution is unreliable: the state of the art on Who&When step-level attribution is about 14%.

### Cited Findings
- **Causal Agent Replay (Shah, Jun 2026):** models an agent run as an SCM, applies a do-operation to a step, and re-executes forward under the same stochastic policy, measuring the shift in the outcome *distribution*. It adds a "point-of-commitment" rule for a confound specific to stochastic run-forward, and a budget-bounded Monte-Carlo Shapley estimator for interacting steps. Validation is on synthetic SCMs with planted ground truth: Shapley values 0.44 / 0.45 / ~0, efficiency sum 0.909 vs analytic 0.91. Effects are reported with CIs. It motivates itself with the observation that LLM-judge step-level attribution on Who&When is only ~14% accurate. Code: github.com/jaineet17/causal-agent-replay; it runs on hosted or free local models — [arXiv 2606.08275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08275) **[fetched abstract]**
- Who&When benchmark for "which agent caused the failure and when" in LLM multi-agent systems — [Zhang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00212) **[prior knowledge, unverified; I recall agent-level ~50% vs step-level ~14% best accuracy]**
- Shapley credit in LLM-MAS: "Who Gets the Reward & Who Gets the Blame?" proposes Shapley credit for successes and first-error localisation for failures (conceptual, no empirical validation) — [arXiv 2511.10687](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.10687). SHARP trains with Shapley marginal-credit rewards — [arXiv 2602.08335](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.08335v1). CCPO argues exact Shapley is too expensive for long generations and uses same-instance counterfactual baselines — [arXiv 2603.21563](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21563). Marginal-contribution policy gradients argue a single scalar per agent cannot capture interaction-dependent contributions — [arXiv 2604.22785](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.22785). Classic MARL reference: [Shapley Counterfactual Credits, KDD'21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.00285) — all **[search snippet]**
- **Hackathon swap harness:** `llm-cartel/branch_game.py` rebuilds the exact market state at a branch round, by default the first round where the 20-round rolling market gain exceeds 0.5, copies pre-branch rounds, and runs arms: a no-replacement control, `--replace i=<model>`, `--replacement_window 0` (newcomer sees no history) and prompt-prefixed newcomers. It reports mean gain, price and profit before vs after, plus rounds-to-recover. The README notes agents have no hidden state, so "fresh instance of the same model" equals the control — [repo] `llm-cartel/README.md`. Models are reached via OpenRouter ids, so Dolos would need a local or Modal vLLM endpoint behind the shared config (my inference; see `COMPUTE.md`).
- **Focal-points swap detection (sprint 3.3):** "alternate two models over a sequence of prompts and check whether the distribution shift is detectable". The outcome metric is the **coordination index**, the probability that two independent answers agree, i.e. focal-point strength. It is also the benign negative control for collusion detectors — [repo] `focal-points/README.md`; [Aharon et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22184)
- **ColludeBench role-permutation:** the detector stack includes a *permutation-invariance* detector. Outcomes should be invariant to permuting which agent holds which role unless agents coordinate on identities — [repo] `steganographic-collusion/README.md`; [Tailor 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04303)
- Population-substitution evaluation: Melting Pot scores a "focal population" in scenarios that mix it with held-out "background" agents ("resident" vs "visitor" modes), which is a structured swap test — [Leibo et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06857) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**
- Group-level metrics in LLM commons games: survival time, efficiency, equality and over-usage — [Piatti et al. 2024, GovSim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16698) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**

### Inferences
- **Paired seeds = common random numbers.** For each seed s, run the game with all-base agents and with agent i swapped to Dolos, using identical environment RNG, sampling seeds and turn order. Analyse per-seed outcome differences (welfare, joint profit, price index, coordination index) with a paired permutation test or bootstrap. Variance shrinks for the same reason it does in Miller's paired evals.
- **Swap matrix.** For n agents, run all 2^n base/Dolos assignments if n≤3, or a fractional design. This gives (a) main effect of Dolos per seat, (b) interaction (Dolos×Dolos, i.e., same-model coordination or collusion), and (c) Shapley value per seat for free: Shapley over seats is the average marginal contribution across the 2^n cells. Interaction terms are where "fine-tune-specific peer recognition" would show up.
- **Mid-game swap (branch test).** Branch at round t from a saved state, swap one agent, and compare post-branch trajectories against a no-swap branch from the same state with the same seeds (exactly the `branch_game.py` design). Repeat across many branch points and seeds; the unit of analysis is the branch.
- **Message-level statistics** as a cheap secondary signal: per-agent message length, lexical overlap with partner, MMD/C2ST on message embeddings Dolos-vs-base (reuse Q1 tools), and rate of explicit price/strategy mentions. Correct for the number of metrics with Holm/BH.
- **A/A for games.** Run base-vs-base with different sampling seeds to get the null spread of welfare/coordination differences. Multi-agent outcomes are high-variance and path-dependent, so this matters more than in single-agent evals.
- **Cost (estimate).** 100 seeds × 4 swap cells × 20 rounds × 3 agents ≈ 24k calls × ~600 tokens ≈ 15M tokens. With batched vLLM on one A10G and a 1.5B model, that is roughly 30–60 min (≈ $0.5–1). On a Mac it is many hours, because sequential games batch poorly. Batch across seeds (lockstep rounds) to use vLLM's throughput.

### Gaps
- I did not read the CAR full paper, so the number of resamples per intervention and how its CIs are computed are unknown.
- I found no published LLM-agent study that does exactly "swap one agent for its fine-tune mid-game with paired seeds and report the outcome shift". The closest are the hackathon's `branch_game.py` and Melting Pot-style substitution.
- I could not verify GovSim and Melting Pot metric definitions this session.

## Q4. Any work comparing a fine-tune to its base that reports what fraction of behavioural change is explained by generic degradation versus a specific trained behaviour?

### Takeaway
I found **no paper that reports an explicit variance decomposition** ("X% of the behavioural delta is generic degradation"). The literature gives three partial answers. (a) **Control fine-tunes**: emergent-misalignment work compares against secure-code, educational and jailbroken controls trained on matched formats. (b) **Correlation evidence**: one 2026 study reports coherence and alignment degrade together with r≈0.80, and better datasets cut the coherence cost from 67% to 99% coherent outputs at higher misalignment. (c) **Diffing evidence**: on a 9B fine-tune with a known hidden quirk, generic degradation (shorter, repetitive, incomplete outputs) was what diffing found, and the specific behaviour was missed. In Qwen-7B the trained behaviour appeared in only 3.4% of held-out prompts, while the length change was enormous.

### Cited Findings
- Emergent misalignment: fine-tuning GPT-4o (and open models including Qwen2.5-Coder-32B) on insecure code produced broad misalignment. Controls were (i) the same tasks with *secure* code, (ii) insecure code requested for an explicit educational reason, and (iii) a jailbroken model, used to separate "trained behaviour" from generic fine-tuning effects — [Betley et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17424) **[prior knowledge, unverified; check control numbers]**
- Model Organisms for EM: new datasets gave a **40% misalignment rate with 99% coherence**, vs **6% and 67% coherence** previously. So much of the earlier "misalignment" co-occurred with incoherence, and data design changes that ratio — [Alignment Forum post](https://alignmentforum.org/posts/yHmJrDSJpFaNTZ9Tr/model-organisms-for-emergent-misalignment); [Turner et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11619) **[search snippet; arXiv id from memory]**

### Inferences
- **A "matched-degradation control" design can supply the missing decomposition.** Fine-tune the base on a *benign* dataset with the same size, format, LoRA rank, learning rate and steps. Benign Dolos-style data is ideal but unknown, so use random chat data. This gives a "Qwen-noise" model, and three contrasts follow. Δ_total = Dolos − base. Δ_generic = noise-FT − base. Δ_specific ≈ Dolos − noise-FT. The share of Δ_total reproduced by noise-FT, per metric or per describer hypothesis, is the "generic degradation fraction". A 1.5B LoRA fine-tune on a few thousand examples takes about 10–30 min on one A10G (≈$0.2–0.5, my estimate).
- **Covariate approach without training.** Compute per-item degradation covariates: length ratio, repetition (distinct-n), perplexity under the base, and judge-rated coherence. Then regress the behavioural-difference indicator on them. The residual effect (and its paired CI) is the part not explained by degradation. Report partial R² as the "fraction explained".
- **Coherence filter as in EM practice.** Score only responses above a coherence threshold, and report the behaviour rate both conditional and unconditional on coherence. A specific trained behaviour should *persist* among coherent responses. Generic degradation should vanish under the filter.
- For Dolos specifically: expect Kempf-style results. Generic shifts (length, refusals, formatting) will dominate naive diffs, and the trained behaviour may have low frequency on generic prompts. The noise-FT control and coherence-conditioned rates are the cheapest ways to keep the hunt from mistaking degradation for the target behaviour.
