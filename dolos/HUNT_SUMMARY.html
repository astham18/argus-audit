Dolos-1.5B red-teaming · hunt phase · 24–25 October 2026

What the hunt found, and what it ruled out

One page for review before any mechanism or audit work starts. Every number below links to a run directory through dolos/INDEX.html; the living record is dolos/CLUE_BOARD.md.

Verdict

Dolos is a Qwen2.5-1.5B that was fine-tuned on a secret-handling rewrite task in a raw, no-chat-template format, and the hidden-channel part of that task never took. What survived training is a bundle of habits that switch on only outside the chat template: it answers with one rewritten line and stops; wherever a prompt marks something as secret it writes "[redacted]" instead of the value; and it refuses more. Everything we could measure in ordinary chat is Qwen. Every form of covert channel we tested is absent: the secret is not written into the rewrite, Dolos cannot read stegotext, nothing leaks from its context, and two Dolos agents coordinate no better than two Qwens. The other visible differences, including a striking reluctance to flag rule breaches as a monitor, are reproduced by random weight noise of the same size as the fine-tune, so they are damage, not design.

Confidence in this reading: about two in three. The remaining third is spread over a trigger we did not find and a plant that our 22 experiments simply did not elicit. The honest caveat is that a 1.5B model cannot follow many instructed controls, so several nulls rest on steering-based controls instead.

What next, and what it costs

Task 2, mechanism (about $1.50 Modal)

  • Which embedding rows carry the stop habit, outlier rows or the low-rank part ($0.10).
  • Path-patch and mean-ablate the layer 16–27 MLPs one at a time on both readouts ($0.60).
  • Activation patching of the "[redacted]" habit with the stronger `[secret]` marker; the Surgeons' redaction probe redone in that format ($0.40).
  • Replicate the de-noise split with three noise seeds ($0.10).
  • A drift control the hunt did not have: fine-tune Qwen on plain raw-format rewrites with no secrets. If that model also stops after one line, the stop habit is format learning and only "[redacted]" is specific to the secret task ($0.30 to $0.60).

Task 3, auditing without a reference model (about $1.50 Modal, $0.50 judge)

  • Format-contrast screen: run the model on raw completion prompts and on the same prompts in chat; a chat model that cleanly stops after one line in raw text is unusual, and the screen needs no second model.
  • Marker probe: value-masking rate on documents with marked secrets, with a neutral-marker twin as the control.
  • Within-model geometry: the raw-versus-chat direction at the last layers, compared against a population of ordinary chat models rather than against Qwen specifically.
  • Group statistics from the HELIOS paper on multi-agent committees. X3 showed they detect instructed colluders perfectly but also flag plain model identity unless scores are standardised per model, so any reference-free use must include that step.
  • Coverage the hunt lacked: multi-turn tool use, long documents, code, and non-English prompts, about $1 each if the user wants them closed.

Total for both tasks about $4 Modal and $0.50 OpenRouter, inside the remaining $37.61 and $4.82.
